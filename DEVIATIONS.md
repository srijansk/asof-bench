# Deviations from the registration

The registration of asof-bench v0.1 is [PREREGISTRATION.md](PREREGISTRATION.md), [labelling/GUIDELINES.md](labelling/GUIDELINES.md), every module in `src/asofbench` (the generator, the stream builder, the materialiser and the scorer, hashed together as `generator.source_sha256` in the manifest) and the generated files the manifest lists, as of the annotated tag `prereg-v0.1.1`. Every change to them after that tag is recorded here with its date, what changed and why, and results that such a change affects are reported both as registered and as amended.

The changes listed below were made before that tag. They are part of the registration that `prereg-v0.1.1` freezes and are listed for transparency; the promise to report results both as registered and as amended applies to the changes made after the tag. No system has answered any item of v0.1, so no run answered a v0.1 item before the commit that fixes its configuration (section 11), the author's `ppr` included, and none is listed here.

## Before `prereg-v0.1.1`

An earlier version of the registration was frozen privately on 2026-10-30 and never published. Its manifest gives the generator's source digest, `generator.source_sha256`, as d0dc63d47738cd099b909a89e511d9b6dea179f5e71a0ea349cb11179545ae72. Since then the registration has changed as follows. Until the change to F1-change of 2026-10-31 (the last entry below), no question, gold value, pilot item, timeline, mention pair or stream changed.

### The systems, their inputs and the runs (sections 8, 11 and 13)

- The network rule: every system runs in an environment from which no network route leads out except to the answering model's API and, for a system whose store a language model builds, the construction model's API; the services the system runs itself stay inside and hold only its own software and models and what it wrote; data/v0.1, every file of this repository but the system's own adapter and configuration, the corpora's live sources, their mirrors and clones are out of reach; provider-side tools are off; and each committed configuration records its network policy. The earlier registration said only that every system receives the same stream and nothing else from the corpora.
- What a system receives: from data/v0.1, each item's question text and nothing else, while the harness keeps the item's id and writes the answer rows; from the corpora, its arm's stream file only, never the files beside it, or what section 11 gives grep at D, long context and the oracle.
- A system may use the question's date through its own interface, such as a temporal filter in its query API, when its committed configuration does so for every item, and the registered retrieval systems (BM25, dense and hybrid) do not.
- Grep at D also receives `git-log.txt`, the log of the commits up to D that `asofbench materialize` writes beside the tree: each commit's id, recorded time, files and message, with no diff and no text.
- Long context reads every episode of the one proposal the question names, those recorded after D included, where it read the episodes of the proposals the question names.
- The oracle answers through the answering model from the statements in effect at the end of the item's date, read from the headers as recorded (in arm CF as the counterfactual stream edits them), and ingests no stream.
- The sensitivity check, claude-sonnet-5-5 on the pilot items, answers over the stores built with claude-haiku-5-5 and is scored in its own run; the earlier registration said only that claude-sonnet-5-5 repeats the pilot items.
- Every answering call sends adaptive thinking at effort medium and no temperature, with either answering model, and the scored runs give the scorer those settings exactly as written; the earlier registration fixed only the default temperature.
- The embedding model is registered: voyage-4-nano, the Hugging Face model `voyageai/voyage-4-nano` at revision 5fb34e870f49f19e79c94d5e118f5ee730133279, run inside each system's environment at its default 2048 dimensions with its `query` and `document` prompts. No system reranks, and Graphiti searches with a reciprocal-rank-fusion recipe. HippoRAG 2's recognition-memory call is made with the construction model in every run, the sensitivity check included, so HippoRAG 2 runs from a commit of its code that keeps the construction model apart from the answering model.
- Under M0's fallback Graphiti is entered as `graphiti-reduced`, an identifier of its own: in every arm it ingests the episodes of the proposals the F1, F2 and CF questions name and the first k other proposals in a fixed hash order, with k the largest number that fits the budget, and it is entered in F1, F2 and CF only; a scoring run that holds both `graphiti` and `graphiti-reduced` stops. It was to run on the named proposals and a disclosed sample of distractors.
- M0's projection covers every ingestion that Graphiti's entries need (the H+, H− and CF streams and section 14's incremental path), from token rates per character of episode text measured on the first 50 PEPs, at the price basis that the manifest records. The Graphiti budget is 500 US dollars, where it was 250, and the price basis's date moved with it: the basis gives claude-haiku-5-5's list prices on 2026-10-31, the day the budget was set, where it gave those of 2026-10-30; the prices it states are the same.
- A scored run is any run that answers a v0.1 item, and no configuration answers one before the commit that fixes it; a run that did would be listed here.
- The full runs answer every item anew, the pilot's included, and the pilot's answer rows are never passed to a full run's scoring.
- The Personalized PageRank retriever, `ppr`, is the author's reference implementation of a published pattern, labelled `ppr (author's own)` in every table; crewlore is not entered because the author develops and promotes it outside this benchmark; and a system that section 11 does not name is scored as an outside entry once an entries file declares it, labelled `<id> (outside entry)` and never compared in the headline.
- Section 11 still leaves out any system built on another benchmark the author co-wrote, but no longer names that benchmark, which is unpublished; section 11 and the README now give the author's competing-interests statement.

### The scorer and the gates (sections 10, 12 and 13)

- The headline comparison: each system that keeps or retrieves context against grep at D on each corpus's H− F1-change items, by accuracy averaged over the runs and the exact two-sided McNemar test on majority results, Holm-adjusted over the 14 registered comparisons with p = 1 for a comparison not made. A comparison is made only where both sides have at least one scored answer; claims come only from the registered full run, and any other run gives readings with its reasons; two readings beside it decide nothing. results.json gives it as `headline`, and every system's per-item results as `items`.
- The gates decide only in the registered pilot run, whose scope holds every F1 item of pilot.json for every pilot system, with the registered models and settings on the registered build. Any other run records every gate's computation with `evaluable` false, its `reasons` and no decision, and counts the pilot's coverage on the pilot items in its scope.
- Gate 1 reads every pilot system on F1-change, and the registration gives the reason for its 85%. Gate 2's "within 10 points" includes a gap of exactly 10. Gate 3 tests for no significant difference, two-sided, against the better of grep at D and long context (against each of them on a tie), where it asked whether Graphiti was not significantly worse. Gate 4 counts the ten pairs of pilot systems without closed book, with Holm-adjusted p-values, where it counted any pair with p below 0.05, and reports the fifteen-pair reading without the adjustment beside it. What the gates would decide on each item's majority result is reported beside the decision.
- Contamination: closed book's per-item majority results are tested against each corpus's F1-change floor by an exact one-sided binomial test that only marks the corpus, and F1-change stays each corpus's primary as-of result; F1-recent was to become the primary result where closed book reached 50% on F1-change. Every system's F1-change accuracy is also split by closed book's result, and its F1-recent accuracy by whether the gold is the status at S and, given `--training-cutoff`, by the answering models' training cutoff, which the registered runs give as 2026-07-01.
- The scorer requires the answering model, the answering settings, the construction model and the embedding model, records them in results.json and gates.json and names them in every heading, refuses `none` where a run's systems need the model, and refuses control, format and separator characters in them.
- Accounting: a row without `tokens_in`, `tokens_out`, `wall_s` or `tool_calls`, or with no number there, is still scored, and each cost figure leaves the missing value out, where the frozen scorer counted it as zero; an Accounting table counts such rows per system and field. The other rules for answer rows were already the frozen scorer's (the last list below).
- results.md gives each group's majority-class rate beside its accuracy; F2 accuracy over the recoverable items, with what always answering the empty list scores; CF items apart by header cue as well as by decision word; H− F1 accuracy apart by a header-only episode after the date and, given `--message-cues`, by message cue; and which companion file it read and whether that file matched the manifest. Every file the scorer writes carries the canary.
- The scorer sums every float exactly, so results.json is the same on Python 3.11 and on 3.12 or later.

### The build, the data and the manifest (sections 7, 8, 9 and 15)

- gold.jsonl's `meta` gains `h_minus_header_only_after_d` and `gold_equals_status_at_s` on the 503 F1 items, `recoverable` on the 82 F2 items, and `header_cue` and `header_cue_fields` on the 30 CF items; the scorer reads these flags and stops on an item that lacks one.
- A CF item's header cue is a last-call-deadline under any of its names, a Superseded-By, Replaced-By, withdrawal-reason or Accepted field with a value, or a Resolution whose case-folded value holds a decision word; 4 of the 30 CF items have one.
- data/v0.1/edges.jsonl publishes the 1,885 header edges at S that relation recall is scored against.
- REPORT.md gains the trivial-answer floors, the counts of the header-only flag, the F2 positives that their own episodes cannot show, and the CF items with a header cue.
- The manifest registers edges.jsonl and, under each stream, the files the stream builder writes beside it, which `asofbench stream` and `asofbench score` check; `source_sha256` is the SHA-256 of the lines that `sha256sum *.py` prints over the generator's regular files, and `source_commit` and `source_dirty` are defined.
- `asofbench build` refuses, before it writes anything, an output directory that already holds a MANIFEST.json or that is the directory of `--expect-pins`.
- The generator and the scorer run on Python 3.11 or later, where the package asked for 3.10 or later; a build under Python 3.11 reproduces data/v0.1 byte for byte, as one under 3.13 does.

### 2026-10-31: F1-change compares each window with the last offered status at S

What changed. F1-change (section 7) compared the status of each eligible window with the proposal's status at S. It now compares it with the proposal's last offered status at S: its status at S when a question offers that, and otherwise the last status a question offers that it held before S, read at the end of each day. A window whose status differs from that status is eligible as before. F1-current, F1-recent, CF and the other families are unchanged, and so are the selection seed, the sizes (150 per corpus) and the hash order.

Why. A Stagnant status records no new decision: EIP-1 moves a Draft, Review or Last Call proposal that has been inactive for six months or more to Stagnant, and moving it back to Draft or its earlier status resurrects it. Because it counted as a change, 80 of the 150 Ethereum F1-change items (73 with the gold Draft, 7 with Review) had as their gold the proposal's last offered status at S, so a system that answers each proposal's current offered status was right on those 80 items whatever the question's date.

Effect. At v0.1's pins, 396 of the 1,229 Ethereum proposals present at S are Stagnant there, and none of the 745 PEPs present at S holds a status that no question offers. The Ethereum F1-change pool falls from 820 proposals to 477, and the PEP pool stays at 635. The Ethereum stratum was drawn again from the new pool in the same hash order: 76 of its 150 proposals remain, 67 of them on the same date, and 74 are new; its gold is now Draft for 98 items, Review for 37 and Last Call for 15, where it was Draft for 116, Review for 28 and Last Call for 6. The PEP F1-change items are unchanged, and so are every item outside the Ethereum F1-change stratum, the pilot's M0 list, timelines.jsonl, edges.jsonl, the mention pairs and the three streams with the files beside them; questions.jsonl, gold.jsonl, pilot.json (its Ethereum F1-change group), REPORT.md and MANIFEST.json change with the new items, and so do the SHA-256s of the registered build that the scorer holds (`REGISTERED_DATA`) and the numbers the registration and the README give for the Ethereum F1-change items.
