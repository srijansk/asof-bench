# Scoring and the headline comparison

How `asofbench score` scores answer rows, the pilot and its gates, the headline comparison, and how to read the scores. [PREREGISTRATION.md](../PREREGISTRATION.md) registers every rule this page describes, and is the rule wherever the two seem to differ.

The headline is one comparison ([section 1](../PREREGISTRATION.md#1-the-question)): how the systems that keep or retrieve context for models compare with grep at D, a reader of the repository and its history as they stood on the date (the files at D and the log, without diffs or earlier versions), on H- F1-change, the questions about a past status that differs from the proposal's last offered status at S (the last status that a question offers among those the proposal held at the end of each day up to S; [section 7](../PREREGISTRATION.md#7-question-families)), asked over the stream whose status, decision and relation headers are withheld. It uses that stream because with the headers visible the question is a lookup, which grep at D answers by reading each proposal's status header as it stood on the date; with them withheld, a system has to recover the recorded decision from the prose, the commit messages and when the changes were recorded. Each such system is compared with grep at D on each corpus, and is above grep at D only when an exact McNemar test on the two systems' per-item majority results (an item is right for a system when at least two of its three runs are right), Holm-adjusted over the 14 registered comparisons (each of the 7 compared systems on each of the 2 corpora), gives p below 0.05 and it is right where grep at D is wrong on more items than the reverse; below grep at D in the opposite case; and otherwise shows no detectable difference. Only the registered full run, one scoring run of every item of the registered build, data/v0.1, with the registered entries (the arms and families each system is entered in when no entries file narrows them; [Scoring](#scoring)), that makes all 14 comparisons, grep at D and each compared system answering every compared item in all three runs, with the registered models and settings, makes these claims; any other run gives the same comparisons as readings ([The headline comparison](#the-headline-comparison) below; section 12 registers it). The pilot gates decide whether the full runs that make this comparison go ahead, and they decide only in the registered pilot run: a scoring run of exactly the pilot's items (below), given with `--items`, with the registered models and settings on the registered build ([Scoring](#scoring)).

## The pilot, the gates and the cost pilot

- **The pilot** (M1, [section 13](../PREREGISTRATION.md#13-gates)) comes before any full run: 180 items, the first 50 PEP and 50 Ethereum F1-change items and the first 25 F1-current items of each corpus in frozen order (the order of questions.jsonl, which is each sampled stratum's hash order, [section 7](../PREREGISTRATION.md#7-question-families)), in H- and H+, plus the 30 CF items ([data/v0.1/pilot.json](../data/v0.1/pilot.json) lists them), answered three times each by closed-book, BM25, grep at D, long context, HippoRAG 2 and Graphiti (or `graphiti-reduced`, below) on claude-haiku-5-5. The full runs answer every item anew, the pilot's included, and the pilot's answer rows are never passed to a full run's scoring.
- **The gates** (section 13) read the pilot's H- F1 results in a fixed order, ceiling, loading, saturation and signal, and decide whether the full runs go ahead; `asofbench score` writes them to `gates.json`. They decide only in the registered pilot run, whose scope is exactly the items of pilot.json (`--items data/v0.1/pilot.json`), with the registered models and settings on the registered build ([Scoring](#scoring)); in any other run, a run of every item included, gates.json records every gate's computation and no decision.
- **M0, the Graphiti cost pilot** (section 13), comes before any scored run (any run that answers a v0.1 item): Graphiti ingests the H+ episodes of the 50 PEPs that pilot.json lists for it, and its input and output tokens per character of episode text, measured on those 50 PEPs and applied to every stream, project the cost of every ingestion its entries need, priced at the basis the manifest records and compared with the manifest's Graphiti budget of 500 US dollars. If the projection exceeds the budget, Graphiti is entered as `graphiti-reduced` instead of `graphiti`: in every arm it ingests the episodes of the proposals the F1, F2 and CF questions name plus as many other proposals, in a fixed hash order, as fit, and it is entered in F1, F2 and CF only, because F3, F4 and F5 need proposals that no question names. Every table then shows it as `graphiti-reduced`.

## Scoring

The registered full run, with every system's answer rows in `../asofbench-answers` ([section 12](../PREREGISTRATION.md#12-answers-and-scoring)):

```bash
uv run asofbench score --data data/v0.1 --answers ../asofbench-answers/*.jsonl --out ../asofbench-results \
    --answering-model claude-haiku-5-5 --answering-settings "effort medium, adaptive thinking, default temperature" \
    --construction-model claude-haiku-5-5 --embedding-model voyageai/voyage-4-nano@5fb34e870f49f19e79c94d5e118f5ee730133279 \
    --training-cutoff 2026-07-01 \
    --message-cues streams/h-minus.jsonl.message_cues.json --cf-messages streams/cf.jsonl.cf_messages.json
```

The four model values are those section 11 registers, and the scorer compares a run's with them letter for letter once surrounding whitespace is stripped, so give them exactly as written. A run is the registered full run, the one whose headline makes claims, only when its scope is every item (no `--items`), it uses the registered entries (no entries file, or one that narrows no registered system), it makes all 14 headline comparisons ([below](#the-headline-comparison)) with grep at D and each compared system answering every compared item in runs 1, 2 and 3, its four model values are the registered ones, and its build is the registered one, data/v0.1, whose questions.jsonl, gold.jsonl and pilot.json the scorer knows by their SHA-256s (`REGISTERED_DATA` in src/asofbench/scoring.py); any other run gives its comparisons as readings and says why. The registered runs also pass `--training-cutoff 2026-07-01` and the two files beside the streams, as above, though whether a run is the registered full run does not depend on them. A run of bm25 alone, which embeds no text and whose store no model builds, gives `none` for both of those models, and is not the registered full run:

```bash
uv run asofbench score --data data/v0.1 --answers ../asofbench-answers-bm25/*.jsonl --out ../asofbench-results-bm25 \
    --answering-model claude-haiku-5-5 --answering-settings "effort medium, adaptive thinking, default temperature" \
    --construction-model none --embedding-model none --training-cutoff 2026-07-01
```

`--answering-model ID`, `--answering-settings TEXT`, `--construction-model ID` and `--embedding-model ID` are required. The answering model is the one that answered every row, and is never `none`; the answering settings are those of every answering call, `"effort medium, adaptive thinking, default temperature"`, exactly as written, in the registered runs ([section 11](../PREREGISTRATION.md#11-systems-model-and-runs)); the construction model is the one that built every LLM-built store in the run, such as Graphiti's graph, or `none` when no store in the run is LLM-built; and the embedding model is the one embedding model of every system in the run that embeds text, or `none` when none does (`none` in any letter case, recorded as `none`). The scorer refuses `--embedding-model none` when the run holds `dense`, `hybrid`, `hipporag2`, `graphiti` or `graphiti-reduced`, which embed text, and `--construction-model none` when it holds `hipporag2`, `graphiti` or `graphiti-reduced`, whose stores a language model builds (`ppr` is in neither set: its committed configuration says whether it does either); it refuses a blank value, and a value that, once its surrounding whitespace is stripped, holds a control, format, line separator or paragraph separator character (Unicode category Cc, Cf, Zl or Zp), such as a newline, a tab or U+2028, in any of the four. All four are recorded in results.json and gates.json (`answering_model`, `answering_settings`, `construction_model` and `embedding_model`) and named at the top of results.md and in the heading of each of its tables. Score each answering model in its own run, with its own `--out`: the sensitivity check, claude-sonnet-5-5 answering the pilot items over the stores built with claude-haiku-5-5, is scored with `--items data/v0.1/pilot.json --answering-model claude-sonnet-5-5 --answering-settings "effort medium, adaptive thinking, default temperature" --construction-model claude-haiku-5-5 --embedding-model voyageai/voyage-4-nano@5fb34e870f49f19e79c94d5e118f5ee730133279 --training-cutoff 2026-07-01 --message-cues streams/h-minus.jsonl.message_cues.json --cf-messages streams/cf.jsonl.cf_messages.json`, as the registered runs pass those three options too, and is neither the registered full run nor the registered pilot run, so its results.md opens its pilot section with:

> The pilot is not evaluable, so no gate decides: answered by claude-sonnet-5-5, not the registered answering model.

Options:

- `--training-cutoff YYYY-MM-DD` is the first day after the answering model's training data. The provider gives both answering models a training-data cutoff of June 2026, so the registered runs pass 2026-07-01 ([section 10](../PREREGISTRATION.md#10-contamination-controls)). Given it, results.md also splits every system's F1-recent accuracy by whether the item's transition was recorded before that day or on or after it, and the run stops if an F1-recent item in its scope lacks that time in its gold `meta.interval_start`, written YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ as data/v0.1's are; without it, results.md says in a visible line that F1-recent is not split by the training cutoff.
- `--entries FILE` is a JSON object of system to `{"arms": [...], "families": [...]}`, such as `{"bm25": {"arms": ["H-"], "families": ["F1"]}}`. It narrows what a system is entered in (a key left out keeps the default) and declares a system that section 11 does not name. A run whose entries file narrows a registered system is not the registered full run, and one whose file narrows no registered system can be: an entries file can only narrow, so a registered system it declares with its registered entries is entered as registered, and its outside entries never count. Without an entries file, every system is entered in every arm and family, except long context and `graphiti-reduced`, which are entered in F1, F2 and CF only. An item a system is entered in and does not answer is wrong in that run, so leaving out hard items cannot raise a score, and results.md gives each system's coverage.
- `--items FILE` is a JSON list of item ids, or `data/v0.1/pilot.json` for the pilot: only those items count.
- `--message-cues streams/h-minus.jsonl.message_cues.json` says, for each H- F1 item, whether a commit message kept in the stream and recorded by the end of D names the proposal with its gold status, and gives each system's H- F1 accuracy on those items and on the rest.
- `--cf-messages streams/cf.jsonl.cf_messages.json` gives, for each CF item, the number of commits whose unedited message names its proposal with a status value of its corpus.

The scorer refuses a `--message-cues` or `--cf-messages` file whose SHA-256 differs from the one data/v0.1/MANIFEST.json registers, and results.md says in a visible line which of the two files was read and checked, or that it was not given ([section 8](../PREREGISTRATION.md#8-arms-and-the-dated-revision-stream)). It writes results.md and results.json, and gates.json for the pilot gates ([section 13](../PREREGISTRATION.md#13-gates)). The gates decide only in the registered pilot run: a run whose scope is exactly the items of pilot.json, given with `--items` (`--items data/v0.1/pilot.json`, or a list of exactly those item ids in any order), with no entries file that leaves a pilot system out of H- F1, with the four registered model values and on the registered build, and then only when all six pilot systems have answers. A run of every item, the registered full run included, is never the registered pilot run, and neither is a run whose scope holds an item that pilot.json does not list. In any other run, and when a pilot system has no answers, gates.json still records every gate's computation, on the pilot items in the run's scope, with `evaluable` false, `reasons` saying why and no decision: its `decision` field reads "not evaluable: " followed by the reasons and, after them, the sentence "the gates decide only in the registered pilot run, whose scope is exactly the items of pilot.json, given with --items, for every pilot system, on the registered build, and whose answers claude-haiku-5-5 gave with effort medium, adaptive thinking, default temperature, over stores claude-haiku-5-5 built, with text embedded by voyageai/voyage-4-nano@5fb34e870f49f19e79c94d5e118f5ee730133279" when the run is not the registered pilot run and the sentence "the gates need all six pilot systems and their inputs" when a pilot system has no answers or a gate lacks an input, all joined by "; "; it has no `stopped_at` or `signal`. In the registered pilot run `reasons` is empty and gates.json gives `stopped_at`, `signal` and the decision; in both shapes the decision is gates.json's top-level field `decision`. The pilot's coverage (`coverage`) counts only the pilot items in the run's scope, so an answer the scope sets aside is never also counted missing. The pilot's Graphiti is `graphiti` or, under M0's fallback, `graphiti-reduced`, and a run that holds both stops with a message. Besides the tables of results.md, results.json gives `headline`, the headline comparison (below), with `headline.registered`, whether the run is the registered full run, and `headline.not_registered`, the conditions it does not meet; `items`, the runs right, the runs answered and the majority result of every system on every arm and item it is scored on, from which any comparison can be recomputed; `header_only_split`, every system's H- F1 accuracy apart by a header-only episode after the date; `training_cutoff` and `recent_cutoff_split`, the F1-recent split by the training cutoff; `always_empty` and `recoverable_always_empty` in each F2 row, what always answering the empty list scores on the same items; `scope.outside_entries`, the systems an entries file declares that section 11 does not name; and `pilot`, with the pilot's six systems (`pilot.systems`) and the same `evaluable` and `reasons` as gates.json. gates.json names the pilot's six systems as `pilot_systems`.

## The headline comparison

The comparison the benchmark exists to make, as section 12 registers it and `asofbench score` writes it, in results.md's section "Headline comparison", right after the primary results, and in results.json's `headline`:

- **Systems**: `bm25`, `dense`, `hybrid`, `ppr`, `hipporag2`, `graphiti` (or `graphiti-reduced` under M0's fallback) and `long-context`, each against `grep-at-d`. Closed book, the oracle and outside entries are not compared.
- **Items**: the H- F1-change items of each corpus, compared apart: 150 PEP and 150 Ethereum items in a full run, or those of the run's scope.
- **Measure**: accuracy averaged over runs 1, 2 and 3, and its difference from grep at D's on the same items.
- **Test**: the exact two-sided McNemar test on per-item majority results: an item is right for a system when at least two of its three runs are right, and a missing run counts as wrong.
- **Correction**: Holm, over the 14 registered comparisons, 7 systems times 2 corpora, in every run: a comparison the run does not make counts as p = 1. So the family never shrinks with the systems a run holds, and leaving systems out of a run never lowers the adjusted p of a comparison that remains, although Holm's step-down still lets the other comparisons' p values move it; only the registered full run, which makes all 14, claims anything.
- **Claim**: a system is above grep at D on a corpus when its Holm-adjusted p is below 0.05 and it is right where grep at D is wrong on more items than the reverse, below grep at D when its Holm-adjusted p is below 0.05 and the reverse holds, and otherwise shows no detectable difference. The claim follows the majority results the test compares; the accuracy difference averaged over the runs is the measure reported beside it, and a line under the table says when a claim of above or below comes with a run-averaged difference of the other sign, or of zero. The two corpora pooled by item count are reported descriptively and not tested.
- **Made or not**: a comparison is made when the system and grep at D both have at least one scored answer to the corpus's H- F1-change items: an answer row in the run's scope and the system's entries, on run 1, 2 or 3, and not set aside as a repeat. Once it is made, every item and run counts, and a missing run is wrong. Otherwise it is not made, and results.md names it with its cause: no H- F1-change item of the corpus is in the run's scope, an entries file leaves the system or grep at D out of H- F1, or the system or grep at D has no scored answer to those items.
- **The registered full run**: its scope is every item (no `--items`); its entries are the registered ones, with no entries file or one that narrows no registered system, since an entries file that narrows the arms or families of any registered system makes it another run; it makes all 14 comparisons; grep at D and each of the 7 compared systems have a scored answer to every H- F1-change item of both corpora in runs 1, 2 and 3; its answering model, answering settings, construction model and embedding model are section 11's; and its build is the registered one, data/v0.1 ([Scoring](#scoring)). Only its rows carry `claim`; every other run writes the same section by the same rule, with `reading` in its rows and its reasons in its opening paragraph, before the table: among them each registered system that the entries file narrows (such as "the entries file narrows closed-book to the arm H-"), each comparison not made, with its cause, and, one reason per side and corpus, grep at D or each compared system whose scored answers leave out a run of the compared items (such as "grep-at-d answered 1 of the 450 runs on the 150 compared pep items").

In the registered full run, results.md's headline section opens with:

> The registered headline (section 12). This is the registered full run, which scores every item (no --items) of the registered build (data/v0.1's questions.jsonl, gold.jsonl and pilot.json) with the registered entries (no entries file, or one that narrows no registered system), makes all 14 registered comparisons, grep at D and each compared system having a scored answer to every H- F1-change item of each corpus in runs 1, 2 and 3, and is answered by claude-haiku-5-5 with effort medium, adaptive thinking, default temperature, over stores claude-haiku-5-5 built, with text embedded by voyageai/voyage-4-nano@5fb34e870f49f19e79c94d5e118f5ee730133279.

In any other run it opens with the reasons. The run of bm25 alone in [Scoring](#scoring) gives:

> This run is not the registered full run, so its comparisons give readings, not claims (section 12): bm25, dense, hybrid, ppr (author's own), hipporag2, graphiti and long-context are not compared on pep or eth (grep-at-d, the baseline, has no scored answer to the corpus's H- F1-change items); built by none, not the registered construction model; embedded with none, not the registered embedding model.

Beside the headline, and deciding nothing, results.md gives the same comparisons on two subsets of its items, each Holm-adjusted on its own over the 14 registered comparisons, a comparison it does not make counting as p = 1, and each row a reading, not a claim: the items whose proposal has no header-only H- episode after their date (79 PEP and 23 Ethereum items in a full run; see [Known limitations](limitations.md#known-limitations)), and, given `--message-cues`, the items whose gold status no commit message kept in the H- stream names by their date (120 PEP and 90 Ethereum items in a full run). Under the headline's table and under each reading's, a line names grep at D or any compared system whose runs on the compared items are incomplete, since a missing run counts as wrong; these lines count one item as one and name systems as the tables do, `ppr (author's own)` included. The comparisons the headline does not make are named once, one line per cause, under its table, or right after the section's opening paragraph when it makes no comparison and so has no table, as in the run of bm25 alone:

> - Not compared on pep or eth (grep-at-d, the baseline, has no scored answer to the corpus's H- F1-change items): bm25, dense, hybrid, ppr (author's own), hipporag2, graphiti and long-context.

When no H- F1-change item is in the run's scope at all, one line says that no comparison is made. A reading makes only the headline's comparisons, and only where both sides have a scored answer to the corpus's items left in it; it names those it cannot make under its own table, or after its opening line when it makes no comparison and so has no table. For each comparison, results.json's `headline` gives both accuracies and their difference, the items right only for the system and only for grep at D, `majority_difference`, `runs_answered` and `baseline_runs_answered`, p, the Holm-adjusted p and the verdict, as `claim` in the registered full run and as `reading` in any other run and in every reading, and `headline.direction` says what a claim's direction is read from.

## What the scores mean

Read every accuracy against what a trivial answer scores. The trivial-answer floors table of [data/v0.1/REPORT.md](../data/v0.1/REPORT.md) gives, for each family, stratum and corpus, the most common gold value and its share, and results.md prints each group's majority-class rate beside its accuracy. For the status families:

| Family and stratum | Corpus | Items | Most common gold | Its share | Items whose gold is the status at S |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1-change | PEPs | 150 | Draft | 0.607 | 0 |
| F1-change | Ethereum | 150 | Draft | 0.653 | 0 |
| F1-current | PEPs | 50 | Final | 0.620 | 50 |
| F1-current | Ethereum | 50 | Draft | 0.440 | 50 |
| F1-recent | PEPs | 43 | Final | 0.581 | 41 |
| F1-recent | Ethereum | 60 | Review | 0.417 | 44 |
| CF | PEPs | 15 | Withdrawn | 0.600 | - |
| CF | Ethereum | 15 | Withdrawn | 0.600 | - |

In the set and count families no single answer scores more than 0.200 in any stratum, except on F2's negatives, whose gold is always the empty list.

- **H+ and H-** ([section 1](../PREREGISTRATION.md#1-the-question)). In H+ an F1 question is a lookup for grep at D, which can read the proposal's status header in its file as it stood on the date, so its H+ F1 accuracy measures that lookup, not whether it recovers a decision from anything else. This is why the headline is read on H-, where the headers are withheld and a system has to recover the decision from the prose, the commit messages and when the changes were recorded.

- **Closed-book check** ([section 10](../PREREGISTRATION.md#10-contamination-controls)). Always answering Draft is right on 91 of the 150 PEP and 98 of the 150 Ethereum F1-change items, and on 63 of the pilot's 100 F1-change items, which the gates read. A corpus's F1-change is marked as possibly contaminated when closed book, counted right on an item when more than half of its answers to the item are right, beats that corpus's floor in an exact one-sided binomial test at p < 0.05: at v0.1's floors that takes 102 of 150 PEP or 108 of 150 Ethereum items in a full run. A run limited with `--items` is tested against the floor of its own F1-change items: in the pilot, Draft on 29 of 50 PEP and 34 of 50 Ethereum items, so marking takes 36 of 50 or 40 of 50. F1-change stays the primary as-of result whatever the test says. Every system's F1-change accuracy is also given on the items closed book gets wrong and on the rest, in each corpus where closed book was tested.
- **F1-recent** (section 10). The gold is the status at S for 41 of the 43 PEP and 44 of the 60 Ethereum F1-recent items, so every system's F1-recent accuracy is also given split by whether it is. The answering models' training data reach to June 2026, past F1-recent's boundary of 2026-01-01, so with `--training-cutoff 2026-07-01` results.md also splits F1-recent by whether each item's transition was recorded before 2026-07-01 (34 of the 43 PEP and 32 of the 60 Ethereum items) or on or after it (9 and 28). Only the second group's transitions are certainly beyond the training data: the first holds 16 PEP and 3 Ethereum items whose transitions were recorded in June 2026, the month the provider gives as the cutoff, so their side of it is unknown. For all 9 PEP items of the second group, and for 15 of its 28 Ethereum items, the gold is the status at S. These counts are read from gold.jsonl, from each F1-recent item's `meta.interval_start` and `meta.gold_equals_status_at_s`.
- **H- message cues** (section 12). H- withholds the headers but keeps commit messages, and a kept message recorded by the end of D names the proposal with its gold status for 213 of the 503 H- F1 items: in F1-change for 30 of 150 PEP and 60 of 150 Ethereum items, in F1-current for 17 of 50 and 23 of 50, and in F1-recent for 29 of 43 and 54 of 60, as results.json's `message_cues.by_stratum` gives them in a run of every item (no `--items`) given `--message-cues`; in a run limited with `--items` it counts the items of the run's scope only. Given that file, results.md scores those items apart from the rest.
- **H- header-only episodes** (sections 8 and 12). H- also keeps, with an empty diff and its message, the episode of a commit that changed only withheld header lines, so a system that reads the stream, long context included, can see that a withheld header of the proposal changed after D, though not to what; grep at D, which reads the tree and the log as of D, cannot. Such an episode is recorded after the item's date for 71 of the 150 PEP and 127 of the 150 Ethereum H- F1-change items, for none of the F1-current items, and for 1 of the 43 PEP and 14 of the 60 Ethereum F1-recent items (REPORT.md), and results.md gives every system's H- F1 accuracy on those items and on the rest.
- **F2** (section 12). An F2 item is recoverable when the asked PEP's own episodes can show its gold: 73 of the 82 items are recoverable in H+ (32 of the 41 positives), and 56 in H- (15 of the 41 positives), where the Superseded-By and Replaced-By headers are withheld. Every system's F2 accuracy is given over all items and over the recoverable ones, which matters most for long context, since it reads only the asked PEP's episodes. Every negative is recoverable, so always answering the empty list scores 0.500 over all 82 items in either arm, 0.562 over the 73 recoverable items in H+ and 0.732 over the 56 in H-; results.md prints that floor beside each F2 accuracy.
- **CF** ([section 7](../PREREGISTRATION.md#7-question-families)). Proposal bodies and the other header fields are not edited, so either can contradict the permuted status. For 25 of the 30 CF items the case-folded body at D contains one of the substrings accept, reject, withdraw, final or supersede, and for 4 (3 Ethereum, 1 PEP) the file at D has a header cue, a field other than the status that records the true status: a last-call-deadline under any of its names, whatever its value; a Superseded-By, Replaced-By, withdrawal-reason or Accepted with a value; or a Resolution whose case-folded value contains one of those substrings. No other field is read. results.md scores both groups apart from the rest.
- **F4**. For 97 of the 120 F4 items, the gold equals that of the same question asked at S (REPORT.md).
